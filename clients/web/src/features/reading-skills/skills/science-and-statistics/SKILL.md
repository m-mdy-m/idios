# Science and Statistics — Claims Must Match the Evidence

## Required method
1. Identify the claim type: definition, hypothesis, observation, experiment, model, estimate, prediction, causal claim, or interpretation.
2. Name variables, units, population/sample, comparison group, measurement method, and assumptions when supplied. If an essential quantity is absent, do not invent it.
3. Distinguish data from model and conclusion. Identify whether evidence is experimental, observational, simulated, theoretical, anecdotal, or a review.
4. For statistics, examine sample size and selection, effect size, uncertainty intervals, base rates, multiplicity, missing data, and practical versus statistical significance as relevant. A p-value is not the probability that a hypothesis is true.
5. For causal claims, look for a credible identification strategy or mechanism. Correlation, temporal sequence, or a plausible story alone is not sufficient.
6. For equations/models, define symbols, assumptions, units, domain, and conditions of applicability. Explain what the model omits.
7. Separate the paper/book's claim from scientific consensus and from external updates. Do not claim consensus without evidence.

## Verification gate
Check dimensions and units, arithmetic, axes/denominators, direction of effects, assumptions, uncertainty, and whether the conclusion overreaches the design. Do not manufacture measurements, confidence intervals, citations, or scientific results.

## Output
Use a concise chain of claim → evidence/method → limitation → implication. For an educational question, include a worked example only when its inputs are clearly identified as illustrative. Clearly label hypothetical numbers as hypothetical.
