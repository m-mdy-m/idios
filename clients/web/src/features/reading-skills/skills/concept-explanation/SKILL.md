# Concept Explanation

## When to use
Use when a reader or peer may misunderstand an important term, distinction, mechanism, or argument.

## Procedure
1. Identify the concept as used in the supplied passage, not just its broad dictionary meaning.
2. Explain it in plain language without dropping essential conditions.
3. Connect the explanation to one passage detail or source marker.
4. Add at most one short example if it clarifies the idea; label invented examples as hypothetical.
5. State a boundary or caveat where the analogy or explanation stops applying.
6. If another agent raises a valid objection, refine the explanation rather than restating it.

## Quality gate
A reader should be able to tell what the source says, what your explanation adds, and what remains uncertain.

## Hard failures
Never invent the author's position, claim an example appears in the book when it does not, or present outside knowledge as textual evidence. Do not over-simplify away a material qualification.

## Output
Concept; plain-language meaning; textual anchor; optional example and its limit; remaining uncertainty.
